## This week's focus

We begin by exploring sound in two directions. Sometimes sound emerges
naturally from authentic pleasure or sensation. At other times, allowing sound
to come first can open the way for enjoyment and feeling to follow. There is
no correct sequence. The invitation is to notice what is true for you.

Pauses for integration are essential. Move slowly, giving yourself time to
notice what is happening rather than rushing from one technique to another.

## Guided practice: Expanding on Receiving vs Taking

This short practice builds on the previous week's exploration of receiving
and taking. Continue noticing your own responses, preferences and boundaries
as you explore touch and sensation.

## Guided practice: Sacral to Vocal Opening

This practice invites you to be present with sensation through slow, conscious
touch. You might explore gentle circles across the sternum and belly, breathing
in and allowing the exhale to become sound. The same quality of attentive
presence can be explored around the womb, ovaries, pubic bone or yoni, always
within your own boundaries.

Notice what emotions or sensations arise. Allow space for feeling, releasing,
breathing and sounding, without needing to make anything happen.

## Exploring touch, breath and sound

You are invited to explore gentle self-touch and massage techniques, including
breast practices such as circles, infinity movements, Earth Fountain, Heaven
Shower and the Deer Exercise. Let breath and sound accompany the movement,
keeping the pace spacious and responsive.

As you explore, you might ask yourself:

- What types of touch does my body respond to?
- Where does my body naturally want more?
- What textures, speeds or qualities feel nourishing?

You may also bring this awareness into a Shakti meditation, allowing feeling,
breathing and sounding to support your experience.

## Your home practice

**Conscious self-touch**

Set aside a little time to explore conscious touch through your hands, arms
and face. Move slowly and stay curious about the sensations that arise.
Breathe with the experience, and allow sound if it feels natural.

There is no need to complete a sequence or reach a particular outcome. Leave
space between moments of exploration to pause and integrate.

## A gentle reminder

Sound does not need to be loud, expressive or continuous. Stillness is part
of the practice too. Let your body set the pace, and take the pauses you need.
