<h1>Awakening Sensation</h1>

<p class="course-lead">
  This week is an invitation to slow down and rediscover the language of
  sensation. Through conscious touch, breath and embodied exploration, we
  begin noticing what it feels like to receive, what it means to take pleasure,
  and how these experiences are different.
</p>

## This week's focus

We will explore the Wheel of Consent through the simple practice of waking
the hands, bringing awareness to the distinction between **receiving touch**
and **taking pleasure**. Beginning with the hands, you can gradually include
the arms and face, exploring touch in a way that feels curious, gentle and
self-directed.

Rather than adding lots of variations, allow the practice to remain simple.
Notice how your experience changes when you receive touch compared with when
you actively take pleasure from the sensation. Let your breath support the
exploration, and give yourself time to pause and integrate.

## Guided practice: Waking Hands

This guided embodiment practice explores receiving versus taking through
the Wheel of Consent. You are invited to notice the difference between
receiving touch, taking pleasure and recognising what each experience feels
like in your body.

## Womb breathing: Opening the breath and sound

This breathing practice invites you to imagine the inhale as water filling
the lower cauldron, gently bringing life and spaciousness into the womb and
pelvic bowl. On the exhale, imagine a soft mist rising along the spine, moving
upwards one chakra or MCO point at a time.

Allow the breath and imagery to remain comfortable. There is no need to force
the breath, create a particular sensation or follow the visualisation perfectly.

### Practice rhythm

- **2 minutes:** Relaxing the pelvic bowl.
- **4 minutes:** Womb-to-spine loop breathing.
- **2 minutes:** Sounding the exhale with a gentle “haaah” or “ooooh”.
- **2 minutes:** Stillness, noticing any residual flow.

## Your home practice

**Daily practice: Pelvic breathing**

Take a few moments each day to bring your awareness to your womb and pelvic
space. Breathe gently into this area, imagining it expanding with the inhale.
Allow the exhale to soften, without pushing or straining.

You can also revisit the simple waking-hands exploration, noticing the
difference between receiving touch and taking pleasure. Keep the practice
slow, connected to your breath and guided by your own comfort.

## A gentle reminder

Your experience does not need to look or feel a particular way. You can pause,
change the practice or stop whenever you choose. Let sensation unfold at your
own pace.
